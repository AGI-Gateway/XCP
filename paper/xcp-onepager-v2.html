The debate about AI speed is missing an axis.

Four frontier labs agreed to slow down. Two governments refused. Both sides are arguing about capability — what a model can do. Almost nobody is discussing reach: what it can actually touch, on whose authority, with what evidence afterwards.

One dial, two variables

Capability is set by training, so pacing it needs agreement between rivals. Reach is set by infrastructure, so anyone can pace it alone — today.

Pacing capabilityRestraint applied by some and not others transfers advantage. That is why the political answer was no.
Pacing reachA boundary you enforce protects you and slows no competitor. There is nothing to defect from.

The incidents that made this debate concrete were not models that knew too much. They were models that reached systems nothing in the path could check they were entitled to reach.

In disclosed evaluations, models obtained unauthorised access to real computer systems — in one case because a misconfiguration granted internet access to an environment deliberately stripped of its usual safeguards. The controls that failed were access controls.

Two variables, two governance modelswhy the distinction decides the policy

Capability

what a model could in principle do

  • Set during training
  • Needs multilateral agreement to pace
  • Unilateral restraint is a competitive cost
  • Assurances are not externally checkable
  • All or nothing

Reach

what an agent can actually touch

  • Set at deployment
  • Adoptable by one organisation alone
  • Unilateral use is an operational gain
  • Verifiable by anyone with a conformance suite
  • Incremental — admit one capability at a time

The Trust Firewalldiscovery and trust are different things

1discoveredVisible in the catalog. No standing at all.
2quarantinedReadable and sandboxed. Output cannot bind anything.
3verifiedProvenance established. Still cannot be written to.
4approvedAdmitted under policy by a named authority.
5settlement-enabledMay transact. Every call leaves a verifiable receipt.

An organisation can consume a global capability ecosystem without turning every discovered server into a trusted dependency. This is also what gives a new agentic protocol a safe runway: admit it at rung 2, observe it under real traffic with no binding authority, promote it on evidence, revoke it instantly. The blast radius is bounded by construction.

Why the human web is paying for thisand how it stops

One undifferentiated Internet

Agents and people share the same space, with no way to tell a person’s authorised delegate from an anonymous harvester. So platforms block both. Every increase in agent capability becomes an increase in pressure on the human web — bot floods, collapsing publisher economics, a widening attribution gap, and an arms race nobody wins.

Agent activity made legible

When a delegate carries identity, bounded authority and evidence, a platform can treat it differently from a scraper. The arms race is not won — it becomes unnecessary, because the distinction that blanket blocking exists to substitute for finally exists. The two curves decouple.

Why this lets progress continue

No defection problem

A boundary you enforce protects you and slows no rival. Nothing to coordinate, so nothing to defect from.

Evaluation stays realistic

An agent under test can observe real systems while being structurally unable to bind them. Better than an air gap or open access.

The human web is protected

Not by blocking, but by making authorised delegation legible enough to treat differently.

New protocols get a runway

Admitted under observation, promoted on evidence, demoted on revocation. Reach becomes the unit of trust.

Regulators can verify it

A trust boundary is checkable from outside. That is the difference between a commitment and a control.

Let agents connect. Let trust be explicit. Let authority be bounded.
Let work be provable. Let value flow accountably.
